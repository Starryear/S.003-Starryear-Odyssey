## Permitted visual translation

Use the following broad qualities when the source photograph supports them:

- monumental scale with a small human or navigation marker;
- tactile real-world materials rather than glossy fantasy surfaces;
- low, close, or strongly directional foreground structure opening into a vast distance;
- restrained earth, sea, smoke, iron, bone, wax, and weather colors derived from the source photograph;
- mythic danger represented through physical pressure, occlusion, scale, and incomplete forms;
- chapter-specific artifacts abstracted into geometry: rope tension, sail curvature, oar cadence, hull ribs, woven thread, bow arc, cave mouth, eye ring, teeth rhythm, or island edge.

Do not default to desaturated blue/orange grading, black title cards, anamorphic flares, actor silhouettes, or trailer typography. Those would turn a source-led art page into imitation.

## Observed lighting families

Public official footage and production stills show more tonal variety than a single “dark epic” look:

- **Daylight coast and departure:** broad pale or clouded skies, steel/sea blues, sun-struck sand and weathered timber, readable armor/rope/ship texture, and long-distance atmospheric depth.
- **Golden-hour monumental action:** warm sun touches large practical structures and people while the environment remains fully readable; warmth is not confined to tiny orange points.
- **Firelit interiors and mythic darkness:** localized flame reveals nearby material, smoke, stone, timber, and water; darkness surrounds the event but is not a featureless black fill.
- **Bleak Hades/liminal landscapes:** muted earth, black sand, smoke, low sky, and broad middle-gray separation create desolation without crushing all shadow detail.

Transfer these as lighting logic, exposure range, real material response, and depth. Do not reproduce a particular shot, composition, costume, creature design, or color grade.
